# Tail dependence in commodity carry

Reading Patton's copula textbook again. The recurring practical problem in our commodity carry book is that linear correlations look fine right up until they don't, and then they're catastrophically wrong.

**The setup**: long backwardated commodities, short contangoed ones. Carries positive on average. Linear correlation with broad equity markets is roughly 0.1 — looks like a clean diversifier. The pitch deck loves it.

**The reality**: in the bottom 5% of equity returns, the correlation isn't 0.1. It's 0.7. The strategy *appears* uncorrelated until the moment when uncorrelation would actually matter, then collapses into the same trade as everyone else. This is tail dependence in action. The Pearson correlation is a single number that averages over the whole joint distribution; it tells you nothing about whether the worst quintile of one variable is informative about the other.

**The diagnostic**: compute conditional probability that strategy returns are below the 5th percentile *given* equity returns are below the 5th percentile. If that conditional probability is materially above 5%, you have tail dependence regardless of what the linear correlation says. For our commodity book, it sits around 30%.

**Why this matters operationally**: portfolio construction tools default to mean-variance optimisation under Gaussian assumptions. The optimiser will happily allocate to a tail-dependent strategy because it sees a low correlation and a positive Sharpe and concludes "diversification." It can't see the conditional structure. So the portfolio looks great in normal times and falls apart in crises — exactly when diversification was supposed to save you.

**Calibration parallel**: this is the same problem as a probe that distinguishes truthful and confabulated outputs in calm conditions but breaks under adversarial prompting. The probe and the truth-detector have *good* unconditional performance — bad *conditional* performance under stress. The diagnostic that matters isn't average performance; it's worst-quintile performance.

**Action item**: revise the regime-conditional copula model. The current one assumes a Clayton copula in the lower tail. Empirical fit suggests we should be using a t-copula with low df — fatter tails on both sides. Estimated parameters logged in the desk repo.
