# Rotation cost vs alpha tradeoff memo

Memo for the desk allocator. We're carrying too much rotation cost relative to expected alpha on the mid-cap sleeve. Math:

Current sleeve characteristics:
- Annualised gross alpha estimate: 380 bp
- Annualised turnover: 380%
- Effective transaction cost (impact + spread + market-on-close fees): ~95 bp on the turnover
- **Net alpha after costs: ~285 bp**

Optimal-frontier reweighting (lower-turnover variant):
- Annualised gross alpha estimate: 290 bp
- Annualised turnover: 180%
- Effective transaction cost: ~45 bp
- **Net alpha after costs: ~245 bp**

The high-turnover variant *appears* to dominate on net alpha. But two things mute that conclusion. First, the transaction cost estimate at 380% turnover has a fat tail — actual costs in stress days are 2× the model. Second, the high-turnover variant has worse capacity (the same percentage degradation hits a smaller AUM range).

**Dynamic conclusion**: when AUM is below $400m, run the high-turnover variant. Above $400m, run the lower-turnover variant. The breakeven is sharper than the marginal-alpha math suggests because the impact cost component grows superlinearly with size.

**Meta-point**: we keep treating "alpha" as if it's intrinsic to the strategy. It's not. Alpha is conditional on the implementation regime — turnover, capacity, market depth. A strategy that produces 400 bp at $100m may produce 50 bp at $1B. The strategy is the same; the alpha is a function of how you run it.

**Same shape elsewhere**: in horse training, the "training value" of an exercise is conditional on the horse's current state, the time of session, the previous exercise sequence. Same exercise on a fresh horse produces something completely different from the same exercise on a tired one. We never write down "this drill produces 380 basis points of training value" because we know it's nonsensical without the conditional structure. We should have similar humility about strategy alpha.
