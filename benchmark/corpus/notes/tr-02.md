# Japan itinerary draft 2026

Three weeks, late September into mid-October. Robin's first trip to Japan; my fourth. Drafting the route.

**The constraints**:
- Robin doesn't enjoy crowds. Tokyo first-timer pull is real; her tolerance for it is ~3 days.
- Both of us want at least a week in genuinely rural Japan — Tohoku or Kyushu, not Kyoto.
- I have an obligation to be back in Brisbane on Oct 14 for a quarterly review.
- Late September is just past peak typhoon risk but still warm enough for outdoor work. Late October is leaf-peeping crowds in the popular spots — should be avoided.

**Working itinerary**:
- Sept 23–25: Tokyo (Yanaka and Nishi-Ogikubo neighbourhoods, deliberately not Shibuya/Shinjuku centred — quieter base, day trips into the louder bits as energy permits).
- Sept 26–29: Tohoku — Sendai → Yamadera → Akiu Onsen. Genuinely rural by Tokyo standards. Reachable by Shinkansen in 90 minutes.
- Sept 30–Oct 4: Kyushu — Fukuoka → Yufuin → Aso volcano → Kagoshima. Onsen-heavy.
- Oct 5–7: Naoshima (the art island). Robin's only "must" of the whole trip.
- Oct 8–11: Kanazawa and the Noto peninsula. Traditional crafts, slower pace, recovery from earlier intensity.
- Oct 12: travel day back to Tokyo.
- Oct 13: Tokyo, last day, low-key.

**Risk register**:
- Typhoon week could collapse the Kyushu segment. Backup: redirect to Hokkaido, accept the regional flight cost.
- Robin's energy. The pace front-loads the high-stimulation parts and back-loads recovery. If Tohoku turns out too intense, we can collapse Kanazawa and add days back.
- Quarterly review schedule slips. Marcus has indicated Oct 14 is firm but knows about the trip.

**Accommodation philosophy**: same as Tasmania — book ends locked, middle flexible. Tokyo (3 nights), Naoshima (3 nights — limited availability so must book ahead), and the final Tokyo night locked. Everything else 2-week flex window.

**Pattern from earlier trips**: the part of every Japan trip I most underestimate the *volume* of is food — not the cost, the cognitive load of constantly choosing what to eat among too many good options. This time, plan to bias toward neighbourhood ramen / izakaya defaults rather than chasing the highest-rated places. Lower-stakes eating means more energy for everything else.
