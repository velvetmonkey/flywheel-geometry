# Solar feed-in tariff renegotiation

Current feed-in tariff is 7c/kWh from Origin Energy. Was 12c when the system was installed in 2022. The tariff has stepped down twice. Wholesale electricity has not stepped down by the same margin. Net effect: I'm getting a worse deal each year while my neighbours' new installs lock in better rates than my old one.

**The mechanics of why this happens**: retailers offer high feed-in tariffs to acquire customers, then quietly step them down at contract renewal. The customer notices late or not at all. The economic surplus migrates from the customer (who can't easily switch given the lock-in costs) back to the retailer.

**My options**:
1. **Switch retailer**. AGL and EnergyAustralia are currently offering 11c. Switching cost: ~$80, annoying admin. Annual benefit on my export volume: ~$540. Clear win, except for the residual concern that the new rate also steps down within 18 months.
2. **Negotiate with Origin**. Threaten to switch, see what they offer. Ratchet effect on my side: they tend to match competitors when faced with a credible exit threat.
3. **Stay put, do nothing**. Lazy default. Costs ~$540/year more than option 1. Real money over 5 years.
4. **Get a battery**. ~$8,000 install. Stops me feeding cheaply-bought-back power into the grid; I store it and use it overnight instead. Payback period 6-7 years at current tariffs; less if grid prices rise.

**Decision tree**:
- If I get a satisfactory negotiated rate at Origin: stay (option 2).
- If Origin won't move: switch to AGL (option 1), revisit the battery question separately.

**What this exercise revealed**: I've been on autopilot for the energy bill for two years. The dollar amount per month is small enough that it doesn't trigger attention; the cumulative cost over a decade is the price of a decent overseas trip. The lesson generalises — recurring small costs slip below my attention threshold and accumulate. Worth a quarterly "audit the small recurring costs" review.

**Connection back to investing**: this is the household-finance analogue of the management-fee drag on a fund. 1% per year sounds small. Over 30 years it's most of your terminal wealth. Same shape — the small recurring leakage that's not salient enough to fix but compounds into the dominant cost. The discipline is the same: notice the drag, fix it, redirect the saved cash to actual returns.

**Action item**: schedule a 1-hour quarterly review of all recurring costs. First Sunday of each quarter. Audit subscriptions, utilities, insurance, fees. Compare to alternatives. Switch where the saving is worth the friction.
