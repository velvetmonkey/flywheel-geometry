# Tasmania trip 2025 — debrief

Two-week trip in February. Robin and I drove the loop — Hobart → Freycinet → Bay of Fires → Cradle Mountain → Mt Field → back to Hobart.

**What worked**: the loose itinerary. Booked accommodation only for the first three nights and the last two. The middle week we played by ear depending on weather. This let us extend Bay of Fires when conditions were perfect (got two days of glassy-water swimming) and skip parts of the west coast when the rain set in. A locked itinerary would have had us in pouring rain at Cradle Mountain for a day we'd already paid for.

**What didn't**: underestimated drive times. Tasmania looks small on the map; the actual roads are slow. A 200 km segment took five hours twice. Lost confidence in Google's time estimates and started padding by 50%.

**The accommodation discovery**: the eco-cabins near Mt Field were unexpectedly the highlight. Off-grid, no Wi-Fi, fed by a small hydro system from a creek. The forced disconnection produced more conversation than the previous five days of "interesting things to look at." Robin and I have agreed the next trip we book includes at least 3 nights with no connectivity.

**The drone**: the Mini 4 Pro paid for itself just on the Bay of Fires footage. The colour palette there is unreal — orange lichen on grey granite, transparent water, white sand. Some of the best aerial work I've shot.

**The journal pattern**: I made evening notes every day of the trip. Reading back, the days I noted feeling "centred" correlate with the slow days, and the days I noted feeling "rushed" correlate with the over-scheduled ones. Surprised this surprised me. Note for future trip planning: build in rest days every third day, not every fifth.

**Cost**: roughly $5,400 for the two of us, including flights. Tasmania is genuinely cheaper than mainland alternatives once you're there; the airfare is the swing factor.

**For the next trip**: Iceland in 2027 is the working idea. Robin wants whales; I want horses (the Icelandic riding holiday literature is a whole sub-genre I want to read). The loose-itinerary approach probably doesn't transfer to Iceland's distances and weather; might need more booking ahead, less freedom on the ground.
