# Daily — 2026-05-04

Long weekend. Used the morning to actually do the insurance comparison spreadsheet I've been putting off for two months. Three takeaways:
1. Contents premium has crept up enough that switching insurer pays for itself by month two.
2. Horse mortality cover at 4% of insured value annually is structurally bad value at Caspian's age. Decision: drop mortality, keep liability and vet, redirect the saved premium into a sinking fund.
3. The drone's scheduled-item cover is over-paying by ~30%. Renegotiate at renewal.

About 90 minutes of work that saves multiples of the time invested per year. *Why* do I keep deferring this stuff? Possibly because the leverage isn't visible until I do the math. Once I do, it's obvious. Pre-doing-the-math, it just feels like another chore. Note for the systems library: build a quarterly admin ritual that *includes* small math-based decisions, so the leverage is salient.

Afternoon: long walk along the Brisbane River with Robin. Weather perfect. The river reach near the New Farm peninsula was surprisingly empty — usually it's busier on long weekends. Worth remembering for next year.

Evening: started a draft of the README for the geodesic-retrieval project. Will need to revise heavily — first drafts always have the wrong tone for me, too philosophical. Ben Caughlin's recent reshape of his Flywheel Geometry repo is a good reference point — measurement-first framing, the cosmological frame moved to a separate philosophy.md. I should steal the structure.

Read the next two chapters of *The Dispossessed*. Le Guin's economics on Anarres is the weirdest sustained thought experiment in fiction I've read; the part where the protagonist tries to explain "ownership" to people who literally don't have the concept is the most useful piece of writing about coordination problems I know. Saving a few quotes.

**Reflection**: long weekends are not really for rest; they're for the work that requires uninterrupted hours. I do my best thinking on Saturdays and Sundays. Mid-week is for execution. The shape of my week wants this asymmetry — accept it, design for it, stop pretending I'll do good thinking on a Tuesday at 4pm.
