# Daily — 2026-04-15

Up at 6:10. Went to the arena at 7 — Caspian was unsettled (a new horse arrived yesterday, the herd dynamic is rebalancing). Did 20 minutes of in-hand work to bring his attention back; abandoned the planned ridden session. *Right call* — pushing through wouldn't have produced learning, only resistance.

Drove to Meridian. The morning model run flagged the materials sector for an unusual position size. Spent the first hour validating — turned out to be a stale data feed from one of the alt-data providers. Fixed by switching the feed; sector position normalised. The alarm worked. Kept logs.

Lunch with Robin near Brunswick Street. She's been working on a string-quartet recording in a studio in West End — interesting reflection from her about the difference between *take 1* (raw, often best) and *take 4* (technically clean but loses the spark). Same dynamic as my own writing. The first draft has the energy; the polished one is correct but flatter. Worth thinking about how to keep some of the take-1 quality through revisions.

Afternoon: deep work on the regime-detection ensemble. The Bayesian online changepoint detector is too noisy by itself; the ensemble approach is converging. Spent 90 minutes reading through Adams & MacKay's original paper to understand what's happening when it spuriously flags. The math is clearer when read carefully than when remembered.

Evening: Robin practising scales (she keeps her practice texture *very* different from my work mode — slow, repetitive, almost meditative). Cooked. Read 30 pages of *The Body Keeps the Score*.

**Bed**. The day had structure; not all do. Notice the days that don't and try to engineer them. The reliable cadence — morning movement, deep-work block, light evening — is what makes the cumulative work compound. The days I lose to scattered attention bleed into the next day, and three of those in a row halve the week's output.
