# Regime change detection — notes

Notes from this morning's research session. The systematic equity desk needs a tighter regime detector. The current one — a Markov-switching model on rolling realised vol — is too lagged. By the time it flags a regime change, the change has been ongoing for two weeks and we've already taken the drawdown.

**Three competing approaches I've tested**:

1. **Bayesian online changepoint detection** (Adams & MacKay). Lightweight, gives a posterior over "time since last changepoint." Catches changes faster than Markov-switching. Downside: very noisy on small samples; prone to flagging spurious changes during single-day vol spikes.

2. **Multivariate state-space model with Kalman filter on factor returns**. Catches changes that show up as factor-correlation shifts before they show up in volatility. Works well in backtests. Hideously expensive to estimate online.

3. **Embedding-based**: encode market state as a vector via a pretrained financial language model (sentiment + news + price action), use distance from rolling-mean state vector as a regime-change signal. Surprisingly competitive on backtests. Not robust to the model's training cutoff — degrades fast on novel regimes.

**What I keep noticing**: each method has a different *failure mode* for regime change. The first is too noisy, the second is too slow, the third is brittle to novelty. None is dominant. Combining them with a Bayesian model average actually beats any single one — the wisdom-of-the-ensemble effect.

**Conceptual parallel**: this maps cleanly onto the way Caspian's gait changes during long sessions. There are at least three signals — breathing, contact, footfall cadence — each of which independently is noisy or lagged, but combined give me a reliable read on his state changes. The principle is the same: regime change in a high-dimensional system is best detected by a heterogeneous ensemble of weak signals, not by tuning a single strong one.

**Open question**: when we deploy the ensemble in production next month, what's the right way to *down-weight* a member when its failure mode is active? An adaptive weight is the obvious answer but introduces a meta-regime-detection problem (how do we detect that one of our regime detectors is currently broken?). At some point the meta-meta tower has to terminate. Probably with a circuit breaker.
