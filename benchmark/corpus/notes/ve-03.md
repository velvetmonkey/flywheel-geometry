# Drone (DJI Mini 4 Pro) flight log

Kept casually since 2025. Not all flights logged, just the ones with anomalies or interesting conditions.

**2025-10-12, Stradbroke Island**: 28°C, 12 kt onshore wind, gusting 18. Full battery. Pushed the wind envelope on the coast — Mini 4 Pro is rated for 10.7 m/s sustained, and gusts above that create position-hold deviations. Got useful footage but the video has visible micro-jitter in still hovers. Need to either weight more or move down to a calmer day.

**2025-12-03, Mount Coot-tha**: ridge soaring conditions, attempted some elevation hovers near the lookout. The aircraft handled the lift well but the GPS multipath errors near the radio towers were severe — position-hold dropped twice. Switched to ATTI mode for the rest of the flight. Lesson: ridge proximity = check magnetic interference *before* flying, not during.

**2026-02-19, Tasmania (Cradle Mountain)**: overcast, 4°C ambient. Battery performance noticeably worse — lost ~25% of expected flight time. Cold derate is real and not well documented in DJI's own materials. Plan future cold-climate trips with at least 2× battery margin.

**2026-04-03, Sunshine Coast**: testing the new active track 360 mode against Caspian under saddle in an open paddock. Performance: surprisingly good. Lost lock once when he went behind a tree. Recovered within 3 seconds. Pattern matched the spec sheet within tolerance.

**Pattern across all flights**: the published spec is a *normal-conditions* envelope. The interesting part of operating any precision instrument is mapping the *boundary* of that envelope to the conditions you actually fly in. Same lesson as factor models — the published Sharpe is conditional on the regime in which it was measured. The robust operator works the boundary explicitly, doesn't trust the spec.

**Underrated affordance**: the drone is a calibration tool for my own observation skills. Reviewing footage forces a different kind of attention than being there did. The discrepancy between what I noticed live and what I see on review is a useful diagnostic of where my real-time attention is leaky. Same effect as recording a riding session and watching it back later — both produce humility.
