# Reading the horse's energy — calibration notes

Priya's been pushing me on this for a year. The phrase "reading his energy" sounds woo until you take it seriously as a *measurement problem*.

What I'm actually doing when I "read his energy": integrating multiple low-bandwidth signals over time. Ear position. Whether the lower jaw is soft or set. Whether his back rounds under my seat or stays flat. The depth of his breathing. The cadence of his footfalls. None of these signals individually carry much information. Combined and tracked over time, they're a high-precision posterior over his current state.

The calibration is in *which combination* signals what. A horse can have flickering ears and be relaxed (just attentive). A horse can have set ears and be relaxed (just hot day, conserving energy). The signals are conditionally informative — you have to know the joint distribution.

What I've found is that I'm a fairly bad calibrator on this for the first 10 minutes of any session, much better by minute 30. There's something about the tuning of my attention that takes warmup time. Possibly I'm establishing a baseline distribution against which deviations become legible.

**Risk-model parallel**: the same pattern shows up in regime detection. Static thresholds (volatility above X, correlation below Y) are weak. Conditional thresholds calibrated against a recent rolling baseline are much stronger. My morning model that's been running on yesterday's distribution catches regime changes faster than one re-fit cold every morning. The warmup matters.

**Practical implication**: I should stop expecting clean signal in the first 10 minutes of a ride. Treat that period as calibration, not as work. Note the baseline. Then start asking for things, with the prior in place to detect when something shifts off it.

The deeper point is that "reading energy" isn't mystical. It's just a name for high-bandwidth conditional inference under partial observability. Riding sharpens skills that show up everywhere. My quant intuition has gotten better since I started taking groundwork seriously, and I don't think that's coincidence.
