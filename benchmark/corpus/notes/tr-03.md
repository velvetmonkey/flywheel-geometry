# Iceland horse-trekking 2024 — packing list

Kept this list after the trip. Two-week riding holiday based out of Skagafjörður in northern Iceland. Riding Icelandic horses, four to five hours a day on cross-country trails. Documenting because future me will forget what mattered.

**Riding kit**:
- Two pairs of riding tights (synthetic, fast-drying). Cotton breeches were a mistake — got soaked in light rain and stayed cold for hours.
- Good waterproof riding jacket. The cheap one I packed leaked at the seams; would replace with a Goretex shell next time.
- One pair of long riding boots, one pair of jodhpur boots + half-chaps. Long boots got too heavy in the mid-trip rainy stretch.
- Helmet (own, certified). They had loaners but I prefer my own fit.
- Riding gloves x2. The pair I lost overboard from a horse on day 4 was the better pair, of course.

**Non-riding kit**:
- Quick-dry merino base layers. Three sets, washed in the guesthouse sinks every other day. Merino is the right answer for Iceland — warm when wet, doesn't smell, dries fast.
- Insulated puffer (Patagonia Nano Puff). Compressed into a stuff sack the size of a melon. Worn most evenings.
- Waterproof shell pants — used twice, both times worth the weight.
- Buff / neck gaiter. Ridiculous how versatile this thing is. Sun protection on clear days, warmth on cold ones, sleep mask if needed.

**Tech**:
- DJI Osmo Pocket. Lighter and more discreet than a DSLR; the trade-off in image quality was acceptable. Wished I'd had the Mini 4 Pro then but didn't yet own it.
- Power bank (10,000 mAh) and a multi-port USB charger. The guesthouses had outlets, but everyone needed them.

**What I wish I'd packed**:
- A second helmet liner. After 4 days the original was permanently damp.
- Small notebook + pencil. The journaling habit was just starting; phone notes felt wrong for the experience.
- Painkillers (the riding muscles you use that you've never used before, day three).

**What I wish I'd left at home**:
- Two of the three books I brought. I read maybe 30 pages total. Iceland in summer doesn't have evenings the way other places do — the perpetual daylight kept us outside.

**Food note**: skyr is unironically the best travel breakfast I've found anywhere. High protein, kept us going through the morning rides. Brisbane sells it now — bought regularly since we got back.
