# Dog walker rota — scheduling logic

Robin's mother's whippet (Rufus) lives with us when the parents travel — usually three week-long stretches per year. The dog walker rota during those stretches is a small operational problem worth getting right.

**The constraints**:
- Rufus needs two walks daily, ~45 mins each.
- Both Robin and I work during the day; we can do morning and evening but not midday.
- Lunchtime walk handled by a dog walker (Sammy, freelance, $25/walk).
- Sammy has flexibility but also has other clients; need to book the rota a week ahead.
- The garden is fully fenced so short break-outs during the day are okay.

**The previous mess**: last year I tried to coordinate this ad-hoc by texting Sammy each morning. Hit two cancellations because Sammy was already booked. Switched to a weekly rota emailed every Sunday — fixed the coordination problem but introduced a different one: cancellations on my side (last-minute work meetings) created friction because Sammy had blocked the slot.

**The current system**:
- Sunday evening: send Sammy the week's slots (Mon–Fri 12:30 walks).
- Wednesday morning: confirm the back half of the week (Wed pm + Thu/Fri).
- Cancellation policy: full payment if I cancel within 24h, 50% if 24-48h, free if more than 48h. Built in to acknowledge that Sammy's time has block-out cost.

**What worked unexpectedly well**: writing down the cancellation policy made cancellations rarer. The discipline of looking at the calendar 48h ahead — to avoid the cancellation fee — produced more accurate calendar review than I'd otherwise do. Small skin-in-the-game forces small attention.

**What I keep noticing**: scheduling problems are usually constraint-satisfaction problems pretending to be people problems. The person dimension matters but the constraint dimension is more tractable. Once I had the constraint structure clear (Sammy's block-out cost, my cancellation rate, Rufus's walk requirements), the rota fell out of the constraints almost automatically.

**Connection elsewhere**: this is the same shape as scheduling research priorities. The "I should write this paper" decision is constraint-satisfaction (time, energy, deadline pressure, opportunity cost of alternatives) wearing the costume of a personal-priority decision. Treating it as the constraint-satisfaction problem it actually is produces faster, less guilty decisions than treating it as a values question.

**For the next dog-sitting stretch (April 2027)**: build the same rota a week in advance, as before. Add: a backup walker contact in case Sammy is sick, vetted in advance.
