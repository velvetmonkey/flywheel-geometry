# Quant track vs ML research — fork analysis

The decision I keep deferring. Ten years in systematic equity has been good — Meridian is a solid shop, the work is intellectually live, the comp is fine. But the *next* ten years feel uncertain in a way the last ten didn't.

**The quant track from here**:
- Promotion path is clear: senior researcher → desk lead → CIO of strategy. Three to five-year horizon to the desk lead level.
- Comp continues compounding well. $X to ~$2X over 5 years if I execute.
- Skill compounding *in the field* is real. I'm noticeably better at this than I was at the 5-year mark.
- Risk: factor-decay dynamics mean systematic equity at firms my size becomes structurally harder over time. The desk-lead role at year 35 may not be doing what the desk-lead role at year 25 does.

**The ML research track**:
- Possible entry via interpretability or RL for finance. Goodfire, Anthropic, OpenAI all have non-academic research roles.
- Massive credential gap. No PhD, no published papers (yet — the geodesic work could become one). Means starting at IC level despite seniority elsewhere.
- Comp would drop materially in the first 2-3 years. Meridian's RSU + performance comp doesn't translate.
- But: the 10-year skill-compounding curve is at the start of the steep section, not the flat. The thing I learn in year 1 of ML research is much more than the thing I learn in year 11 of systematic equity.
- Risk: I might just be wrong about my own ability to make the transition. Mid-career switches are notoriously survivor-biased — the success stories you read are not a fair sample.

**The hybrid path** (least defined): keep the day job, ship the geodesic-retrieval bench publicly, see if anyone in the ML research world reaches out. Convert the interest into a part-time advisory or sabbatical-research role, then re-evaluate. Optionality preserving.

**What Marcus said when I floated this**: "The question isn't which track has higher expected value. Both are reasonable lives. The question is: which version of you do you want to be at 60? You answer that, the path picks itself."

**Working answer**: hybrid path for 12 months. Ship the bench. See who notices. Re-evaluate at the year mark.

**The discomfort I keep noticing**: this is a long-cycle decision being made under heavy short-cycle noise (recent quarter at Meridian was great → "stay!"; a frustrating week at Meridian → "leave!"). The decision quality has to come from the structural reasoning, not from how this week went. Hard to internalise.
