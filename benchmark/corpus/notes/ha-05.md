# Insurance renewal comparison spreadsheet

Annual insurance review. Building this comparison spreadsheet because each year I forget the methodology and have to reconstruct it.

**The portfolio**:
- Home contents (replacement value ~$140k)
- Building insurance (paid via body corporate, not directly)
- Polestar 2 — comprehensive
- Trek Powerfly — added rider on contents (more on this below)
- Caspian — third-party liability + vet/mortality cover
- Robin's instruments — separately scheduled on the contents policy
- Drone — covered as scheduled item
- Travel — annual multi-trip policy

**The comparison method**: rather than comparing premiums, compare *premiums divided by claim limit* per item. A policy with a $500 deductible and $50k limit on jewellery is fundamentally different from one with $0 deductible and $30k limit. Headline premium hides this.

**This year's findings**:
- The contents policy has been quietly raising the premium 9% per year while inflation runs ~4%. Real-terms premium has compounded materially. Going to shop this.
- Polestar comprehensive is competitive — three quotes within 5% of each other; staying with current insurer.
- The Trek e-bike rider on contents is *underpriced* by the insurer (they don't fully understand e-bikes' actual replacement cost; my $7k bike is being insured at category rates that assume <$3k). Will not call attention to this. The mismatch is in my favour, will let it run until renewal asks the right question.
- The horse mortality cover is the line item I most underestimated. Premium = 4% of insured value annually. Over a horse's working life that's 30%+ of his value paid in premiums. Consider self-insuring the mortality piece (keep the third-party liability, drop mortality, save ~$1,800/year). Calculation: am I better off paying the premium or holding $40k in a separate sinking fund? Probably the sinking fund — the expected annual cost of mortality below the insurer's loaded premium.

**The pattern**: insurance is a portfolio of small leverage trades on tail events. Each trade has a premium-vs-payout ratio. The headline question — "is the premium reasonable?" — only matters in light of the payout structure. The shopper who looks only at premium gets adverse-selected by the insurer.

**The discipline that produces good outcomes**: a small once-yearly spreadsheet exercise comparing apples to apples. Most people don't do this; insurers love it. The rate at which premiums creep up is bounded only by how much friction it would take you to leave.
