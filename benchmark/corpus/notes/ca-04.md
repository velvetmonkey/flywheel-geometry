# Conference circuit — ROI estimates

Reviewing which conferences are worth attending in the back half of 2026. The default answer ("attend the ones I attended last year") is lazy. Trying to do this with explicit reasoning.

**The question**: what does a conference give me that I couldn't get from reading the proceedings + a few targeted DMs?

**Things conferences provide that text doesn't**:
1. Hallway conversations with researchers I wouldn't have warm intros to. Real, irreplaceable. The structured talks are usually the *least* valuable hour on the conference's schedule.
2. Compressed signal about the field's current taste. What's hot, what's tired, what's quietly being abandoned. Hard to read from papers because papers lag the consensus by 12 months.
3. Forced context-switching out of my daily mental modes. Sometimes the most useful thing about a conference is being away from Brisbane for four days and physically tired enough to think differently.

**Things conferences pretend to provide but don't**:
1. Talks I "couldn't have read at home." For 90% of talks, the recording or paper is sufficient.
2. Networking for the sake of networking. Random small talk produces 1% follow-throughs at best. The valuable connections happen at dinners with already-warm contacts, not at booth-area chats.

**The 2026 candidates and my read**:
- **NeurIPS** (December, San Diego). High signal density on interpretability tracks given the geodesic-retrieval angle. Probably yes; need to angle for at least 2 dinner/coffee meetings with Goodfire-adjacent researchers to make the trip pay off.
- **ICML** (July, Vancouver). Moderate. Less interpretability-focused than NeurIPS. Probably skip unless someone I want to meet is there.
- **Quant industry conference** (September, NYC). I usually attend. This year: skip. The industry conferences have flattened — same speakers, same takes, low surprise. Spend the time on Crucible instead.
- **Local Brisbane ML meetup**. Free. 1 hour/month. Yes — costs nothing, occasionally produces a useful contact.

**The accidental conference**: there's a small interpretability workshop being put on by a Sydney-based researcher in October. ~30 attendees, day-and-a-half format. The signal-to-noise on these tiny events is usually 5× higher than big conferences. Going.

**Pattern**: the right strategy for conferences is the inverse of what most people do. Skip the big-name events with too many people; lean into the small focused workshops where everyone in the room actually cares. Same logic as buying small-cap value vs large-cap growth — less liquid, more mispricing, higher real return.
