# Bhutan trek — visa timeline

Robin's parents are doing a guided trek in Bhutan in spring 2027. Considering joining them for the second half. Capturing the visa logistics because Bhutan is famously different from anywhere else.

**The system**:
- Bhutan does not allow independent tourism (with limited exceptions for Indian / Bangladeshi / Maldivian nationals). Australian passports require a registered Bhutanese tour operator to handle everything — visa, internal travel, accommodation, guide.
- Tourist tariff: $200 USD/day Sustainable Development Fee, plus $100/night minimum hotel. Effectively a $300/day all-in floor. Three weeks is roughly $6,300 just for the SDF + accommodation, before flights or extras.
- Visa application has to be lodged at least 30 days before departure, by the operator, with all itinerary details locked.

**The operator question**: Robin's parents are using Bhutan Eternity Tours, recommended by friends. They have a good track record but their itinerary is fixed — joining for half means accepting their schedule, no flex. Different from how I usually travel.

**Risks**:
- Trek difficulty. The route (Druk Path Trek, modified) goes to ~4,300m. Robin's father has done high-altitude trekking before; I have not above 3,500m. Acclimatisation is a real concern. Will need at least 4 days at intermediate altitude before going high.
- Weather. April is the better window; June onwards is monsoon. Latest forecast feed not great.
- Bandwidth at the right level. Bhutan has unexpectedly good internet in towns, but the trek segments will be offline. Need to align Meridian commitments around a 10-day no-contact window.

**Why consider it at all**: Bhutan is structurally unlike anywhere else I've been. The constraints (mandatory operator, tariff, fixed itinerary) produce a trip experience that is *intentionally* not optimised for the tourist's freedom. That's interesting in a way most travel isn't. You're meeting a system that's set up to preserve something specific about the place rather than to maximise your enjoyment.

**Connection back to investing**: this is the same dynamic as a fund with deliberate capacity constraints — Renaissance Medallion, et al. The constraint isn't a flaw; it's how the system preserves what makes it work. Brisbane property market with no foreign-buyer constraints would behave differently from Brisbane with them. Bhutan with no tariff would behave differently from Bhutan with one. Constraints can be features, not bugs.

**Decision**: 60% likely to go, conditional on the altitude profile and the work calendar.
