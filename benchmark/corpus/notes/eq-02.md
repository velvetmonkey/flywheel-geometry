# Lateral work session 2026-04-12

Worked shoulder-in down both long sides, then alternated shoulder-in / travers in 10m circles. 50 minutes total. Priya supervising.

**What worked**: the rhythm-first approach. Once I stopped trying to angle Caspian before establishing tempo, the lateral movement happened almost by itself. The cue I think is "create the shoulder-in" is actually "create the rhythm cleanly enough that the shoulder-in is the path of least resistance."

**What didn't**: shoulder-in to the right is still notably worse than to the left. He hollows on the right rein, drops the right shoulder, and rushes. Working theory: I unconsciously brace through my right hip when riding right, which transmits as conflicting cues. Priya thinks it's me, not him.

**Pattern observed**: there's a regime change in his way of going around minute 25. Before that, he's offering compliant but mechanical work. After 25 minutes of correct rhythm and consistent cues, he switches to *offering* — anticipating the next shoulder-in, lifting his back, taking the contact forward. Same horse, same exercises, completely different feel. The transition is sharp, not gradual. Something about cumulative consistency tipping him into a more engaged state.

**Connection point**: this maps onto something I see in factor portfolios. Sometimes the position works mechanically for weeks — small positive carry, no surprises — and then suddenly the regime shifts and the same position starts paying differently. Same trade, same exposure, completely different return character. The shift is sharp, not gradual. In both cases, the lever isn't the action — it's the consistency of conditions that causes a phase change in the underlying dynamics.

**For next session**: address the right-rein bracing. Maybe ride with no contact on the right rein for 5 minutes to feel where I'm interfering. Priya suggested off-horse stretching for the right hip flexor too.
