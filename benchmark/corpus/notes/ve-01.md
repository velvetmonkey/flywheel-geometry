# Polestar 2 — service log

Bought 2024. Long Range Single Motor. White, black trim. Currently 38,400 km.

**Service intervals** (per Polestar Australia's recommendation):
- Annual: ~$280, mostly inspection + brake fluid + cabin filter. No oil to change. The maintenance footprint is genuinely small relative to ICE — the "cost of ownership" delta over 5 years is real.
- 60,000 km: heat pump refrigerant top-up. Estimated $400.
- 100,000 km: tyre changes (factory Continental EcoContact 6 wear out around 70k for the way I drive — early replacement at next service).

**Energy use**: averaging 17 kWh/100km over the last 12 months. Brisbane heat hurts efficiency in summer (cabin cooling load is real). Winter is fine. Chargers: Wattever home wallbox at 7 kW most nights, public DC fast-chargers (Chargefox) for road trips.

**Anomalies tracked**:
- 2025-11: heat pump warning code briefly. Self-cleared after a software update. No service action.
- 2026-02: regen braking felt sticky for a week, then resolved. Tracked but no action — could have been temperature-related.
- 2026-04: Bluetooth pairing issue with my Pixel 8 after Android update. Standard reboot resolved.

**Pattern**: the maintenance regime is mostly about *telemetry interpretation*. The car streams a constant signal of "everything is fine" and the maintenance question is whether any deviations from baseline are noise or warning. So far they've all been noise. The problem is that an actual warning would look like noise initially.

**Calibration parallel**: this is the same diagnostic problem as detecting regime change in a market. Both are "telemetry stream with low base-rate of true signal." The temptation in both cases is to over-fit to noise patterns and either get spooked by every flicker or miss the genuine warning when it arrives. The middle path is rolling-window statistical baselines and a willingness to accept that some noise stays noise.

**For next service**: ask Polestar to print the high-voltage battery state-of-health. Want a real number, not just "healthy."
