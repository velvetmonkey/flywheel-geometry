# The shape of a long bet

A bet you'll know the outcome of in six months has a different shape from a bet you'll know in ten years. Six-month bets compound feedback fast — you make many, learn quickly which heuristics work, the loop is tight. Ten-year bets compound differently — you make few, the feedback is slow and contaminated by everything else that happens, and the bet's quality is more about its *internal coherence* than about your live calibration.

Most career-defining decisions are ten-year bets. Most professional skill is built on six-month bets. There's a misalignment.

**What helps for long bets that doesn't help for short bets**:
- Pre-registering what would convince you you were wrong. (Six-month bets, you'll just see the answer. Ten-year bets, you'll rationalise around any outcome unless you wrote down the falsifier in advance.)
- Worrying about your motivations more than your reasoning. (Reasoning errors get caught by feedback in short bets. In long bets, motivated reasoning is the dominant failure mode and there's no live correction.)
- Optionality over conviction. (A long bet that doesn't admit course correction is a coin flip with extra steps. A long bet structured as a sequence of revisable smaller bets is a learning system.)

**What helps for short bets that hurts long bets**:
- Confidence calibration based on recent track record. (Recent record on six-month bets says nothing useful about a ten-year bet's quality.)
- Adjusting strategy based on intermediate signals. (Intermediate signals on long bets are usually noise that triggers panic.)

**Connection to investing**: the conflation of these two horizons is the most common mistake in junior PMs. Treating a five-year-thesis position with the same risk discipline as a five-day trade either over-trades the conviction position or under-trades the short-cycle one. The temporal horizon is the dominant variable; the strategy wrapper is secondary.

**Connection to relationships**: the same dynamic applies. Daily interactions are short-cycle bets — fast feedback, course-correctable. Long-term commitments are long-cycle bets where the live feedback is mostly noise and the quality lives in the structural fit you established at the start. Mistaking one for the other is how good marriages collapse over small things and bad ones survive on momentum.

**For the project list**: the geodesic-retrieval bet is sitting in an awkward space. The empirical falsifier is short-cycle (ship the bench, see if numbers land). The framing is long-cycle (geometry as a primitive). Need to keep these horizons clearly separated in the work or one will eat the other.
