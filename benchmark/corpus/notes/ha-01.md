# Quarterly tax estimate Q1 2026

Australian PAYG instalments + the side-income reconciliation. Doing this myself this quarter rather than handing to the accountant — partly to keep tax-current cognition active, partly because the side income from a small consulting engagement adds complexity my accountant takes a week to bill for.

**Income components** (Q1 2026):
- Meridian salary + Q4 bonus (paid in Q1) — tax already withheld, mostly straightforward.
- Small consulting fee from a Melbourne fund — paid as ABN income, GST applicable. ~$8,400 incl GST.
- Investment dividends — Australian sourced, franking credits to capture.
- Foreign dividends from one US ETF — withholding to claim back via the tax treaty.

**Estimated PAYG instalment**: ATO calculator gives a number based on prior year's tax. The number this quarter is misleading because the consulting income won't continue at the same rate. Need to vary the instalment using the variation form, otherwise I overpay and chase the refund later. Decision: vary downward by 30%.

**The franking credit detail**: most retail investors miss this. Australian dividends carry franking credits — pre-paid tax at the corporate level. When I receive the dividend, I can claim the franking credit against my own tax liability. On a $1,000 fully-franked dividend, I get $1,000 cash + ~$430 franking credit, taxed on the $1,430 grossed-up amount. Net effect: dividends are taxed at marginal-minus-corporate-rate, much friendlier than they appear at first glance.

**The conceptual point**: Australian tax has a dozen of these "look at the actual mechanics, not the surface number" gotchas. Foreign withholding, franking credits, division 7A, CGT discount thresholds. None of them are conceptually hard once you've worked through them once. The accountant value-add is mostly in *remembering* them when fatigue is high — not in the conceptual difficulty.

**Connection back to model design**: same shape as factor portfolio reporting. The headline returns hide implementation details (transaction costs, capacity constraints, tax drag) that materially change the picture. Reading only the headline is exactly the same kind of mistake as ignoring franking credits. The mature operator looks past the surface number every time.

**For next quarter**: build a simple spreadsheet that does the variation calculation automatically. The arithmetic isn't hard; the discipline is. Automate the discipline.
