# Reading list — strategic thinking

Building this list slowly. The goal is books I'd recommend to someone earlier in their career who wanted to develop better long-horizon judgment, not "leadership."

**Already read, kept on the active reread list**:
- *Seeing Like a State* — Scott. The single most useful book I've read on the costs of legibility. Applies directly to risk modelling: every legible model imposes a partition on the underlying reality, and the partition has costs the model can't see.
- *Reality Has a Surprising Amount of Detail* — Mollick (essay). Short but I reread quarterly. The argument: the gap between "knowing a thing" and "doing a thing" is usually filled with details that are invisible until you try.
- *Antifragile* — Taleb. Yes, the man's tone is what it is. The framing of "what gains from disorder" still earns its keep when thinking about risk.
- *On the Heart* — Hadot's collected essays. Stoic philosophy as practice rather than doctrine.

**Currently reading**:
- *The Body Keeps the Score* — van der Kolk. Slow read. Mainstream but useful for the somatic-attention thread I keep returning to. Connects to the riding-as-attention-training arc.
- *Probably Approximately Correct* — Valiant. Foundational, occasionally heavy. Worth working through carefully.

**Working queue**:
- *Range* — Epstein. Heard the argument secondhand many times; want to read primary.
- *The Knowledge Illusion* — Sloman & Fernbach. On the social distribution of knowledge.
- *Trust* — Lou Marinoff (philosophical, not the pop one). Thicker treatment than the management-book version.

**What I keep noticing reading these**: the books that hold up over rereads are the ones with operative *frames* rather than *checklists*. *Seeing Like a State* gives me a way to look at any system; the frame works in domains Scott never wrote about. Whereas books with checklists ("the 7 habits of...") tend to atrophy fast — the checklist either becomes habit and recedes, or doesn't and you stop using it.

**The reading discipline I'm trying to keep**: one fiction book for every two non-fiction. The fiction shapes my prose and my pattern recognition in ways nonfiction doesn't. Currently reading Le Guin's *The Dispossessed* slowly. The economics of Anarres holds up better than most economics textbooks.

**The book I keep promising myself I'll write**: a quant-trader's guide to attention. Not a how-to. A frame. The stoic practice section, the somatic attention texture stuff, the regime-conditional inference parallel between trading and riding. Maybe the geodesic-retrieval project is part of the same arc, just expressed in code instead of prose.
