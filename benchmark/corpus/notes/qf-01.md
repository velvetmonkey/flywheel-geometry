# Meridian Capital — book overview

Refresher for new joiners. Meridian runs a multi-strategy book across four desks. I sit on the systematic equity desk; this note is my mental model of the whole shop.

**Equity systematic** (my desk). Mid-frequency factor portfolio, rebalanced weekly. Standard factor exposures (value, momentum, quality, low-vol) with proprietary tilts on regime-conditional weighting. Capacity: ~$800m. Sharpe target 1.4.

**Macro discretionary**. Smaller team, higher conviction. Trades rates, FX, commodity term structure. Lower turnover, longer holding periods. The book most exposed to regime change — they print or bleed depending on whether the macro environment cooperates. Capacity bigger than equity but more episodic.

**Credit relative-value**. Fixed-income arb across cash bonds, CDS, and synthetic structures. Profits from temporary mispricings between related instruments. Sharpe tends to be very high in calm markets, brutal in stress. Tail-dependence problem: when correlations break down, the entire book moves the wrong way at once.

**Volatility / tail-risk**. Long convexity, structurally negative carry, designed to print when everything else is failing. Acts as a P&L hedge for the rest of the firm. Bleed in calm markets, save the year in stress events.

**Allocation logic**: the firm-level allocator weights desks based on a regime-conditional view. Calm regime → more credit RV, less tail. Stress regime → more tail, less credit. The interesting thing about this allocation framework is that it's *itself* a model that can be wrong about regime detection. The meta-risk is the regime model going stale.

**What I keep coming back to**: the book is structurally robust because each desk fails in different conditions. No single regime kills the firm. But if the desks become *correlated* in their failure modes (e.g., all desks underperform in a liquidity crunch), the structure collapses. That's the non-obvious tail risk: not individual desk drawdowns, but correlation-of-drawdowns.

Same shape as horse training, weirdly. A horse that knows ten exercises is robust if the exercises fail in different conditions; brittle if they all collapse under the same kind of pressure.
