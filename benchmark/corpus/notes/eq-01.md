# Caspian — horse profile

8-year-old Andalusian gelding. Dappled grey, full mane, 16.1hh. Bought from a small breeder in the Lockyer Valley three years ago. Currently stabled at Kenmore Equestrian, working with Priya weekly.

**Temperament**: forward but not hot. Reads handler energy quickly — calibration of my own state matters more than any technique I apply. If I come to the arena tense from Meridian, he picks it up in the first five minutes and we lose the whole session. If I show up centred, he settles within minutes and we get real work done.

**Strengths**: lateral movement (especially shoulder-in and travers), uphill canter, willingness to engage on small cues. Picks up new exercises in 2–3 sessions if introduced cleanly.

**Weaknesses**: tendency to anticipate and offer the previous figure rather than wait for the cue. Hollows under pressure. Cold-back issue in winter — needs a 10-minute walk warmup before any contact, no exceptions.

**Training arc**: started in groundwork and long-lining, worked up to first-level dressage tests. Currently building toward a flying change. The change has been a 6-month project — most of the difficulty isn't physical, it's the calibration of straightness and balance going into the canter aid. Get those right and the change happens almost incidentally.

**The thing I keep relearning**: every time I think I'm training Caspian, I'm also being trained. He's a feedback system that punishes inconsistency immediately and rewards calibrated input. Working with him has changed how I think about quantitative model design. Both are about calibration, both are about reading distributional signals correctly, both have a tail-risk regime where small mis-calibrations compound into refusals or losses.
