# Mechanistic primer on residual streams

Notes from working through the basics. The residual stream is the bus everything else writes to and reads from — attention heads add their weighted-sum contributions, MLPs add their feature transformations, and the final layer norm + unembed translates to logits. Every layer's contribution is *additive* in this sense, which is why steering vectors work at all.

Two things I keep forgetting:
- Layer index matters enormously. Early layers carry surface lexical structure; mid layers carry abstract concepts; late layers carry task-specific output preparation. Pick the wrong layer for a probe and you measure the wrong thing.
- Aggregation is not innocent. Mean-pooling residual stream activations across tokens loses positional structure. Last-token pooling assumes the final token has aggregated everything — usually true for autoregressive models, sometimes false. Decide before you measure.

The thing nobody mentions in the textbooks: when you sum residual contributions you're implicitly assuming a kind of distributional commutativity. Two heads contributing to the same direction is reinforcement; two heads contributing in opposite directions is cancellation; two heads contributing in nearly-orthogonal directions is genuine compositional encoding. Linear probes can't distinguish these cases. You need higher-order structure to see the difference, which is why nonlinear methods keep finding things linear ones miss.

This connects back to a thing Marcus said about factor portfolios: *"the second-order structure is where the alpha lives. First-order is everyone."* Same shape.
